I am a PhD candidate at UdeM/Mila, co-supervised by Eilif Muller and Irina Rish. I aim to understand the conditions under which models lose prior capabilities and gain new ones, in order to predict and influence observable phenomena such as forgetting and plasticity across scale.

By studying the properties of current language models, I aim to connect continual learning to test-time sampling, demonstrating how diverse reasoning patterns can be surfaced as a model continues to learn. I study how the structure and dynamics of intermediate representations propagate to the output distribution as tasks shift, allowing for principled interventions, such as at the data or activation level to mitigate forgetting and promote plasticity. In recent work, [Failed Reasoning Traces Tell You What Is Fixable (But Not by Reading Them)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05145), I show that the distributional signature of a model's failed rollouts, rather than their text, predicts which test-time intervention can rescue a given failure, yielding a training-free routing rule that helps most on the hard-to-fix cases.

Previously, I have led work on benchmarking [prior coding knowledge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12367) and interference, contributed to work on [continual pre‑training strategies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01908), and explored [test‑time learning](https://openreview.net/forum?id=IsbtaJ3IrJ). Throughout all of these projects, the aim has been consistent: find ways to improve models over time and better evaluate them over time. Looking forward, most compute will be spent to solve new problems rather than old ones, so the better we understand these phenomena the more strategically we can allocate compute towards solving the important problems.
