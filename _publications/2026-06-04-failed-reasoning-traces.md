When a language model fails a reasoning problem, some failures are just unlucky sampling that more retries can fix, while others are structural and resist resampling. We show that the distributional signature of failed rollouts (not their text) encodes which test-time interventions can rescue a given failure, yielding a training-free routing rule that improves rescue on the hard-to-fix subset.
